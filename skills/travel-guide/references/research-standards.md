# Research and editorial standards

## Claim classes

Classify claims before drafting:

- `documented`: directly supported by a suitable primary or authoritative source;
- `interpretation`: a scholarly or institutional reading, attributed to its source;
- `tradition`: a community, religious, or literary tradition, labeled as such;
- `promotional`: an operator or tourism-board claim that needs independent support;
- `volatile`: hours, prices, booking, transport, closures, access, and safety rules.

The class controls the wording. Do not silently promote a tradition, interpretation, or promotional claim to documented fact.

## Time layers for historic places

Record four dates or periods separately whenever they differ:

1. foundation tradition or earliest documentary reference;
2. date of the oldest surviving material;
3. major restoration, relocation, reconstruction, or adaptive reuse;
4. creation of the modern visitor attraction or scenic area.

Use precise verbs: `founded`, `documented`, `built`, `rebuilt`, `restored`, `relocated`, and `reconstructed` are not interchangeable.

## Source selection

Match the source to the claim:

- UNESCO or official heritage inventory: designation, boundaries, significance, conservation history;
- museum or archive: collections, excavations, inscriptions, object history;
- peer-reviewed work: contested chronology, archaeology, religious or social interpretation;
- transport operator or attraction: current service, tickets, opening and access rules;
- journalism: recent closures, policy changes, or disputes;
- personal travel report: subjective experience only.

Use at least two independent sources for controversial claims, exact superlatives, or facts that materially affect the trip. If an official page is unreachable, record the limitation and use a reputable institutional mirror or independent authority; never cite only a search snippet for a precise claim.

## High-risk wording

Independently verify or soften:

- oldest, largest, first, only, best preserved, world-famous, or world ranking;
- exact ages unsupported by a dated scientific study;
- “built without nails,” “visible from space,” and similar popular formulas;
- place-name stories tied to famous figures;
- clean causal chains connecting folklore to a festival or historical event;
- claims that a modern replica is an ancient original.

Prefer descriptive value over a fragile superlative.

## Practical information

For volatile facts, record `checked on YYYY-MM-DD`, source, timezone, and applicable season or visitor category. Avoid turning a current observation into a permanent rule. If the trip date is distant, give the verification method and recommend a recheck window.

## Citation hygiene

- Link to the exact supporting page, not a homepage when a direct page exists.
- Keep publisher and page title in the source registry.
- Ensure the cited page actually supports the nearby claim.
- Avoid long quotations; paraphrase and cite.
- Do not allow a single weak source to support several unrelated claims.
