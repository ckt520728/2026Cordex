## Current decisions

- Reader-facing language: Traditional Chinese, Taiwan usage.
- Itinerary page controls attraction scope and sequence, but does not control historical truth.
- Historical claims favor primary or institutional sources.
- Legends and later tourism narratives must be labeled separately from documented history.
- The UNESCO property of Ping Yao includes Shuanglin Temple and Zhenguo Temple, although this itinerary visits only the walled city.
- The Mount Wutai itinerary visits only part of the UNESCO cultural landscape and omits major components such as Foguang Temple.
- The UNESCO Great Wall page contains the obsolete “visible from the moon” claim; do not repeat it.
- “平遙鏢局” remains ambiguous and must be matched to the actual museum before publication.
- The itinerary's claim that Datong Nine-Dragon Wall is the only double-sided glazed dragon wall is wrong; Xinhua identifies the Beihai Nine-Dragon Wall as the only double-sided dragon wall among the three major examples.
- Hanging Temple's founding tradition reaches back to Northern Wei, but most surviving architecture is Ming-period reconstruction with later repairs.
- Datong City Wall must be presented as surviving historic rammed-earth fabric combined with extensive post-2009 restoration, not as wholly intact Ming fabric.
- The present Fish Pond Flying Bridge at Jinci was reconstructed in 1953 according to its earlier form; do not describe it as untouched Northern Wei or Song fabric.
- The itinerary names only a generic escort agency, while the official Pingyao pass lists Tongxinggong, North China First Escort Agency, and China Escort Agency. Keep the guide generic until the operator identifies the actual venue.
- Treat Rishengchang superlatives and heroic claims cautiously. Explain its remittance, branch-accounting, credit, and risk-control system instead of calling it a modern bank without qualification.
- Mianshan's Daluo Palace and Sky Bridge contain major modern scenic-area construction; they are not presented as intact ancient structures.
- The public evidence does not support a precise age for Yunqiu Mountain's cave ice; “ten-thousand-year ice cave” is treated as a scenic name, not a measured date.
- The present Stork Tower was completed in 2002 and is explicitly distinguished from the lost Tang-period tower.
- Xiyagou's hanging road is treated as twentieth-century collective road-building history, not an ancient road.
- Yanqi Lake is treated as a modern reservoir, resort, and international-conference landscape rather than an ancient monument.
